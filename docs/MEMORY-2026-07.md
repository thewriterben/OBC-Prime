# Memory: where OBC stands, and the one thing it should build

Field survey of agent memory as of July 2026, checked against OBC's actual
memory code. Companion to `RESEARCH-2026-07.md`.

---

## Thesis

The agent-memory field has spent two years building **chat memory** — remember
what the user said across sessions — and it is in worse shape than the marketing
suggests. Meanwhile OBC has, almost by accident, built the substrate for a
different and harder problem: **sensor memory**, where beliefs come from
hardware that can disappear.

The literature's own conclusion is that the hard part is not storage or
retrieval. It is knowing **what you still believe and why**, and every shipped
system fails at it. OBC is one database column away from being the first system
to do it properly.

---

## 1. The field is not in good shape

Three findings should calibrate any decision to build in this space.

**The benchmarks are broken.** LoCoMo's honest ceiling is **93–94%** because
roughly 99 of its ground-truth answers are wrong, misattributed or hallucinated;
Mem0 claims 92.5% against it — i.e. statistically at the ceiling of a corrupt
benchmark — while a naive full-conversation baseline scores ~73%. LoCoMo
contains **no knowledge-update questions at all**. On LongMemEval, published
numbers *for the same system* span **49% to 94%** depending on who ran it. One
system claimed 100% on both; an audit found its LoCoMo run used `top_k=50`,
retrieving entire conversations — there was no retrieval step — and its
LongMemEval "100%" measured `recall_any@5` with a runner that never generates an
answer and never invokes a judge.

**Most memory systems are net-negative at realistic scale.** Convomem
(arXiv:2511.10523) finds plain full-context reaches **70–82%** on hard
multi-message cases where Mem0 gets **30–45%**, and full context stays practical
to ~150 conversations. Below that threshold, adding a memory system makes things
worse.

**Cost varies by more than an order of magnitude for the same work.**
arXiv:2606.06448 measured a **26.7× construction-energy spread** across systems
on identical tasks (BM25 582 kJ → Letta 15,429 kJ), 47× variation in energy per
correct answer, and per-query latency from 0.10 s to 22.6 s. Retrieval latency
is nearly flat in store size; essentially all the cost is on the **write** path.

The implication for OBC is not "build a better one." It is: do not compete on
chat memory, and do not trust any published number.

## 2. What OBC already has

Read out of `src/memory/world.rs`. This is stronger than I expected and
stronger than the systems it would be compared against.

### 2.1 Bitemporal facts with supersession

`world_facts(id, entity, value_json, valid_from, valid_to, ingested_at, source,
origin)`. A new observation **closes** the entity's currently-open fact by
setting `valid_to`, then appends. History is preserved, not overwritten.

Only Zep/Graphiti does this among the major systems. Mem0's base tier
**hard-deletes**; only its graph tier marks relations invalid. MemStrata
(arXiv:2606.26511) shows why supersession must be deterministic and keyed rather
than similarity-based: on 98 labelled pairs, **contradictions had *higher* mean
embedding similarity (0.812) than genuine duplicates (0.800)**, capping
similarity-based contradiction precision at 0.667. Cosine distance provably
cannot separate a contradiction from a restatement. OBC keys supersession on
`entity`, which sidesteps the problem entirely.

### 2.2 An origin type that refuses to collapse into one order

```rust
pub enum Origin {
    Observed,    // a sensor, radio, or driver reported it — the world said so
    Derived,     // the framework computed it from other facts
    Asserted,    // an agent concluded it. True or not, it is a claim, not a reading
    Instructed,  // a human said so — authoritative for intent, not evidence
}
```

With `OriginSet` — a **set, not a threshold** — and this note in the source:

> *"May I treat this as evidence about the world?" ranks `Observed` above
> `Asserted`; "does this carry authority to act?" ranks `Instructed` highest. So
> consumers declare the set of origins they accept, never a threshold. A
> consumer that collapsed both questions into one level would be wrong about one
> of them.*

The belief-revision literature's recommendation for agent memory is to rank by
**epistemic entrenchment** rather than Graphiti's and Mem0's flat "newest wins."
OBC does better than that recommendation: it recognises that trust is not a
single ordering, because evidential weight and authority-to-act are different
questions. I have not found another agent memory system that makes this
distinction.

Two further details that are easy to get wrong and are right here:

- **`Origin::parse` fails closed** to `Asserted`. *"A caller that has not thought
  about provenance must never produce evidence."*
- **Origin is decided at the boundary where content enters and travels with it**,
  explicitly because *"it cannot be reconstructed downstream from `source`, since
  a trusted component relaying agent-supplied content writes under its own source
  label."* That is the laundering problem — a trusted relay making untrusted
  content look trusted — solved correctly, and it is the same problem the
  memory-poisoning literature is currently losing.

### 2.3 Why that matters against the poisoning results

MemPoison (arXiv:2607.14651, Jul 2026) measures undefended **Behavioral
Corruption Rate at 54.67–66.87%** across ten models, with **dormant** payloads
the most effective at **76.72%**. Write-time defenses block only 40.19% of even
direct injection. The best available prompt-injection filters run 42.5–67% TPR.

Filters are losing because they try to detect malice in content. Origin typing
doesn't: it records *where the bytes came from* at the boundary, and lets each
consumer declare what it will treat as evidence. That is a structural defense,
and it composes with the taint tracking already in `[safety]`.

## 3. The gap, stated precisely

**OBC records that a fact is `Derived` — "the framework computed it from other
facts" — and records nothing about *which* facts.**

There is no `derived_from`, no justification, no dependency link anywhere in
`src/memory/`. The type of a derived belief exists; its support does not.

So when a source stops existing, OBC cannot answer "what did I believe *because
of* that source?" It has the vocabulary for the question and no edges to walk.

**This is not theoretical. It is the bug we hit** — twice, in two different
shapes, both still open in the live store.

*Corrected 2026-07-28 after querying `world.db` rather than trusting the note I
had written from memory.*

**Shape one — the source was switched off.** `mesh.escalated_count = 2` (row
5151), source `mesh-supervisor`, written 17:57 on 27 July from rows 5149 and
5150. The supervisor was disabled in config at 21:44 the same evening and OBC
restarted a minute later, so nothing has recomputed the count since. It is
hours stale, not weeks — an earlier draft of this document said weeks, which was
wrong for this row. The mechanism is what recurs, not this instance.

**Shape two — the source was made unreachable, which is worse.** Five rows from
the 17 July phantom-node incident are still open ten days later:
`mesh.escalation_status` (an agent note that lexical discovery misread as a
node) plus the `.health`, `.recovery` and `.escalation` facts the supervisor
then computed *about the phantom*. Row 880 still reads `status: "escalated"` for
a node that never existed.

Those four derived rows are not merely stale. The fix for that incident sourced
node discovery at the radio, so the phantom is no longer in the supervisor's
view — which means `ClearEscalation` can never fire for it. The clearing path
was removed along with the bug. Nothing in the system will ever close them, and
nothing records that they were computed from a fact that has itself been
disowned.

That is the difference the in-list makes concrete: the phantom note is still
there to point at, and its dependents are still there to find, but the edge
between them was never written down, so the two facts sit in the same database
with no way to connect them.

Doyle's **JTMS** (*Artificial Intelligence* 12(3), 1979) solved exactly this
forty-seven years ago: beliefs carry justifications with in-lists and out-lists;
when a premise is withdrawn the system performs **dependency-directed
backtracking**, relabelling precisely the beliefs whose support vanished. De
Kleer's ATMS (1986) generalises it to assumption sets.

**No production LLM memory system implements any of it.** Zep/Graphiti's
invalidation is *pairwise and non-transitive* — a fact derived from an
invalidated edge keeps its own `t_valid` untouched. Mem0's DELETE is a leaf
operation. The 2026 survey of agent memory (arXiv:2603.07670) concedes that
temporal versioning, source attribution and contradiction detection are
"recommendations, not current practice."

And the field has now built a benchmark for it without recognising its own
history. **STALE** (arXiv:2605.06527, 2026; 400 expert-validated conflict
scenarios, 1,200 queries, contexts to 150K tokens) separates Type I
co-referential conflicts — two observations updating the same attribute — from
**Type II propagated conflicts**, where "the new observation updates a different
attribute whose consequences cascade to an older belief." Their example is a leg
injury invalidating the stored belief that the user commutes by bike. That is the
JTMS problem, reinvented as a benchmark, and current systems fail it.

*Corrected 2026-07-28 after reading the paper rather than a summary of it.* The
headline **55.2%** is the best evaluated model's **overall** accuracy across all
three probing dimensions — State Resolution, Premise Resistance, Implicit Policy
Adaptation — not a Type II score. An earlier draft placed the number directly
after the Type II sentence, which reads as a Type II result. It is not one, and
no Type II figure is claimed here.

Two details from the paper worth keeping. Its Table 1 scores STALE as the only
benchmark of its kind testing **Cascading Invalidation** — "whether an update to
one attribute can invalidate structurally related attributes" — which is exactly
the `derived_from` walk. And its own proposed baseline, CUPMem, works by
"structured state consolidation and propagation-aware search": the same diagnosis,
approached by inferring propagation at read time rather than recording it at write
time.

## 4. What OBC should build

### 4.1 Justification links, then propagation

One column and one walk.

```
world_facts.derived_from  -- JSON array of fact ids (the JTMS in-list)
```

Populated wherever `Origin::Derived` is written — the framework already knows
its inputs at that moment; it simply discards them. On invalidation, walk
forward transitively and relabel dependents.

Use **how-provenance**, not why-provenance. Green–Karvounarakis–Tannen's
provenance semirings (PODS 2007) give the operative distinction: `a·b` means
both sources were needed (kill either and the belief dies) while `a+b` means
independent corroboration (the belief survives one source's death). Pairwise
LLM contradiction checks and cosine similarity cannot represent this distinction
at all. ProvSQL (VLDB 2018) shows the overhead is generally **under 4×** using
semiring circuits rather than expanded polynomials.

### 4.2 Source liveness as a written fact, not an inference

This is where OBC's position is genuinely unique, and it comes from outside the
LLM literature.

**Prometheus 2.0 solved "the sensor went away" in 2017.** Pre-2.0 used a blunt
5-minute lookback, so alerts kept firing after a target vanished — precisely
OBC's mesh bug. 2.0 introduced **staleness markers**: a reserved NaN bit pattern
appended to the series when a target disappears from service discovery, when a
scrape fails, or when a previously-present series stops appearing. The design
insight is the transferable part:

> **Absence of data is itself a datum, and must be written — distinguishable
> from "not yet arrived" and from "reported false."**

Flink adds the complementary idea: a source must **declare itself idle**
(`WatermarkStrategy.withIdleness`) or one dead partition stalls global progress.
AWS Device Shadow keeps per-attribute freshness metadata so a twin knows which
fields are current and which are merely last-known.

For OBC: when a perception source stops reporting, write a staleness marker for
that source, then walk `derived_from` and mark dependents unsupported. The
`mesh.escalated_count` fact would have been retracted automatically the moment
the supervisor was disabled.

### 4.3 Distinguish undercutting from rebutting defeat

Pollock's distinction, and the reason existing systems either over-retract or
serve stale values: a **rebutting** defeater asserts ¬p; an **undercutting**
defeater says the *inference* no longer holds without asserting anything about p.

"The sensor is gone" is undercutting. The correct state for
`mesh.escalated_count` is not `0` and not `2` — it is **unsupported**. Every
surveyed system collapses these into a single invalidation. OBC's `Origin` type
is already the right shape to carry the distinction.

### 4.4 Why this is OBC's to build and not anyone else's

The chat-memory systems have no sensors. A source that stops existing is not a
problem Letta or Mem0 or ChatGPT memory has, so they have not solved it and have
no reason to. OBC is a sensor-driven agent with bitemporal storage, origin
typing, and a production instance of the failure. That combination exists
nowhere else.

The publishable claim: *dependency-directed belief revision for embodied agent
memory, with source liveness as a first-class signal.*

Not "evaluated on STALE" — an earlier version of this line said that, and
§5 below explains why it cannot be, at least not without building a different
contribution first.

## 5. Secondary gaps, ranked

**Write policy is unsolved everywhere, including here.** OBC has ~21 `observe`
call sites in `agent/mod.rs` alone, 15 in `mesh_supervisor`, more in
`pose_fusion`, `navigation`, `reflex`, `feedback` — with no central admission
policy. "When Not to Write Memory" (arXiv:2607.02579) shows repeated
observations in agent traces are **not independent evidence**: naive
frequency-based promotion gives a **0.597 false-promotion rate**, falling to
0.040 only with dependency-aware governance, at a 69.2% human review burden. On
an external stress test of 133 high-impact candidates, **zero** were safe to
auto-promote. Nobody has a safe autonomous write path. OBC's `derived_from`
would make dependency-aware admission possible, which is the only approach shown
to work.

**No consolidation, and that may be correct.** Sleep-time compute (Letta, Apr
2025), dreaming, reflection and observer/reflector loops are now standard —
*and essentially unevaluated for drift.* No benchmark scores whether a
consolidated summary is faithful to what it replaced. Given that summarisation
producing claims exceeding any source is a documented contamination mechanism,
not having consolidation is currently a safety property. Add it only with a
fidelity check.

**No eviction, unbounded growth.** 56 open facts today is nothing; a live camera
mesh is not. No major system has a principled eviction policy and no benchmark
measures one. Bitemporal history makes this worse — TPC-BiH measured **7–73×
slowdowns** for system-time queries, so history-aware retrieval on every turn is
not free.

**Session memory is a flat 50-message window.** `MAX_HISTORY_MESSAGES = 50` with
no summarisation, no retrieval, no cross-session continuity. Given Convomem's
result, this is defensible for now — but it is why the agent's sense of itself
lives or dies on the system prompt.

## 6. What not to build

- **Not a chat-memory system.** Crowded, badly evaluated, and Convomem says most
  deployments are net-negative under ~150 conversations.
- **Not similarity-based contradiction detection.** MemStrata proves the ceiling
  is 0.667 precision because contradictions are *more* embedding-similar than
  duplicates.
- **Not another benchmark score.** Vendor numbers for one system on one benchmark
  span 45 points. If OBC evaluates, evaluate on STALE Type II and publish the
  runner.
- **Not exact minimal-side-effect retraction.** Deletion propagation is NP-hard
  in general (Kimelfeld, Vondrák, Williams, PODS 2011). Ship an approximation
  with stated behaviour.

## 7. Order

1. ~~**`derived_from` column + populate at `Origin::Derived` write sites.**~~
   **Shipped 2026-07-28.** Three states, not two: `NULL` unknown support,
   `[]` self-standing, `[ids]` supported. `observe()` already defaults to
   `Origin::Derived`, so most rows are typed derived without anyone having
   thought about provenance — a sweep reading `NULL` as "nothing supports this"
   would retract the store on first run. Not backfilled: a wrong in-list is
   worse than an absent one, because the wrong one gets walked.
2. ~~**Source-liveness markers.**~~ **Shipped 2026-07-28.** `stopped()` writes
   the disappearance as a fact at `source.<name>.liveness` — state, reason, and
   when the source last spoke — then retracts what rested on it. `Retired`
   (configured off, deterministic) is distinct from `Silent` (inferred, and
   capable of being wrong about a slow source); only the deterministic half runs
   at boot, where a source that has not spoken *yet* is indistinguishable from
   one that died.
3. ~~**Transitive relabelling on invalidation.**~~ **Shipped 2026-07-28**, with
   one deliberate omission. The walk is breadth-first to fixpoint with a
   seen-set. `a·b` only: an in-list is conjunctive, so a fact with two supports
   is treated as needing both. `a+b` needs a fact to carry *alternative*
   in-lists and the schema holds one — the remaining half of this step.
   `Unsupported` as a distinct state from retracted is also still open;
   retraction is currently expressed by closing the valid-time interval, which
   says "not currently believed" and nothing more, and is correct as far as it
   goes.

   **What this actually taught us**, and it is worth recording because it
   contradicts the confident ordering above: after step 2, support coverage in
   the live store was **0 of 20,431**. The graph existed and was empty.
   Propagation had nothing to traverse, and the mesh bug that motivated the
   whole line of work turned out to be fixable by source bookkeeping alone —
   the supervisor wrote the count under its own label, so retiring the source
   reached it without consulting a single in-list.

   The in-list only earns its keep across a source boundary: a conclusion whose
   author is alive and whose *grounds* are gone. Teaching the supervisor to
   declare its inputs produced that case for the first time — retiring the radio
   now unwinds health → escalation → count, three levels, five beliefs, none of
   them written by the source that died.
4. ~~**Dependency-aware write admission.**~~ **Shipped 2026-07-28**, at the one
   boundary where it mattered most: the agent's own `world_memory` write now
   refuses a no-op restatement. Every write supersedes, so an agent that reads a
   fact and writes it back makes a stale belief look freshly confirmed — by an
   agent that learned it from that same belief. It also defeats liveness
   directly, since an echo lands under `agent` and a silent source the agent
   keeps restating never looks silent.

   The same rule then had to be applied at the *perception* boundary, where the
   volume actually was: the ClawCam poll had written 50 distinct events **9,600
   times**, inflating `vision.count.white_tailed_deer` to 5,418 from 28 real
   detections.

   The harder half of this step — a promotion policy across the remaining ~50
   write sites — stays open, and is bounded by coverage rather than by design.
5. ~~**Evaluate on STALE Type II** and publish the harness.~~ **Scoped out
   2026-07-28**, after reading the benchmark instead of the summary. Recorded
   rather than quietly dropped, because it was the headline of §4.4.

   STALE's input is **dialogue** — up to 150K tokens of multi-session
   conversation, scored by an LLM judge on three probing dimensions. OBC's
   support graph is written by *instrumented producers*: the mesh supervisor
   names the escalation facts it summed, pose fusion names the readings it
   fused. Nothing infers a dependency edge from natural language, and nothing
   here is designed to.

   So scoring OBC on STALE would mean first building an LLM pipeline that reads
   dialogue and emits typed facts with in-lists. That is a separate and larger
   contribution, and the failure mode under test would be *that inference*, not
   the revision machinery — the machinery would be measuring its input.

   The honest position: OBC and STALE diagnose the same defect (their Table 1
   calls it Cascading Invalidation; CUPMem attacks it with propagation-aware
   search) and sit at opposite ends of it. STALE asks whether a model can
   *infer* that a belief has been undercut. OBC asks what to do once a producer
   has *declared* what a belief rests on. A number from one does not transfer to
   the other, and claiming one would have been exactly the kind of borrowed
   figure §1 of this document criticises.

   What would be worth building instead: a harness over OBC's own store that
   constructs propagated-conflict scenarios in the form OBC actually ingests,
   and measures whether `support_status()` and the cascade reach the right set.
   That measures the machinery. It is not a STALE score and must not be
   presented as one.
6. Only then consider consolidation, and only with a fidelity check.

## 8. Beyond the roadmap

Two things shipped that this document did not anticipate, both because the live
store showed them rather than because the survey predicted them.

**Retention.** A fourth way to stop believing something, and the only one that
comes from a rule rather than from the world. Supersession, liveness and
dependency withdrawal all fail to reach an agent's own notes, which stayed
believed for ten days after the investigation that produced them concluded.

**Evidence identity outside memory.** Row identity — the thing `derived_from`
needed — turned out to be what the *reflex engine* needed too, for an unrelated
reason. Debounce is a rate limit, not a novelty test, so a rule watching a
standing condition re-fired forever at the debounce interval: hourly wakes of a
30B reasoner on images from 6 July. A reflex may now require the ids of the facts
satisfying it to have changed.
